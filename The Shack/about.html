Our Story

A motorcycle accident, a pit of pecan wood, a pandemic closure, and a new owner who wouldn't let the legacy die.

View Menu

The Shack BBQ opened its doors on North Frankford Avenue in March 2014, the work of Kyle Farris and his wife Kelly. Farris had spent the year before the opening recovering from a serious motorcycle accident — a month in the hospital and six more weeks learning to walk again — before finally getting the pit fired up. What started as a small operation slinging pecan-wood-smoked plates and sandwiches out of a rustic, tin-sided building grew into one of Lubbock's most talked-about barbecue joints, known for a full bar, live music, and an outdoor patio that stayed busy on weekend nights.

Farris ran The Shack for six and a half years before the pandemic caught up with the business in 2020 — rising brisket costs (over $7 a pound, by his account) and supply chain trouble made it too hard to keep the doors open, and he and Kelly made the decision to close and relocate closer to family. It could have been the end of the story.

"We wanted to keep the legacy alive." — on reopening The Shack after an eight-month closure

Instead, in 2021, new owner Scott Stephenson took over the space and reopened The Shack BBQ, deliberately keeping the original pitmaster, cook, and menu in place to preserve what regulars already loved about the place. Under his ownership The Shack has kept growing: in 2025 the restaurant moved from counter-ordering to full table service with servers, added a complimentary beer, wine, and cocktail bar running Wednesday through Sunday evenings, and extended its hours to better serve the growing North Frankford corridor. A small fire broke out in the pit area in September 2025; Lubbock Fire Rescue contained it quickly and The Shack kept right on smoking meat.

Today The Shack BBQ holds a 4.6-star rating from more than 1,400 Google reviews, with regulars calling out the brisket, the collard greens, and the lively patio scene. Everything is smoked over pecan wood and made fresh daily — when a cut sells out, it's gone until the next day's smoke.
