Our Story

A stay-at-home mom's prayer, a closed-down diner, and sixteen-plus years of chicken fried steak and pie.

View Menu

Cast Iron Grill got its start in 2007, when Teresa Stephens — a Lubbock stay-at-home mom with no restaurant experience and, by her own account, a dislike of cooking — opened the doors of a small 55-seat diner on the corner of 18th Street and Avenue K in downtown Lubbock. She's told the story of that leap many times: a prayer to be used to make a difference, a phone call about a shuttered restaurant, and a feeling that the place was simply home.

Teresa runs the restaurant alongside her husband Shelby and, these days, their grown children and extended family. In January 2013, Cast Iron Grill relocated to its current home at 620 19th Street — a building with its own local history, having once been a truck stop and later the site of the well-known Stubbs BBQ. What started as a 55-seat diner has grown to seat roughly 200 guests, with a staff that has grown right along with it.

"It's all about the boots, pie, and chicken fry."

Regulars know the restaurant almost as much for its décor as its food: pairs of boots hang from the rafters throughout the dining room, each one donated by a guest along with a personal story — a tribute to a loved one, a funny memory, or simply a piece of someone's life. The restaurant calls them "people's memories," and the collection has grown for years.

The kitchen's signature dish is chicken fried steak — hand-breaded and smothered in cream gravy — but the menu has grown well beyond breakfast and lunch classics into full entrees, burgers, salads, and a genuine Texas-sized list of scratch-made sides. Out front, a dedicated bakery case turns out dozens of made-from-scratch pie varieties every week, along with catering trays for offices and events around Lubbock.

Cast Iron Grill also runs a branded food truck for catering and off-site events around the city, and the restaurant earned a TripAdvisor Certificate of Excellence in 2016. Above all, the Stephens family describes the restaurant as a ministry as much as a business — a place built, in Teresa's words, to make a difference in people's lives one biscuit and one slice of pie at a time.
