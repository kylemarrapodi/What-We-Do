Montgomery Township

A 32-square-mile township of old Dutch farmland and new-money cul-de-sacs a few miles north of Princeton, with no single downtown — instead three named villages, Skillman, Belle Mead and Blawenburg, scattered across rolling fields, historic churches and a Route 206 corridor that does most of the everyday business. Montgomery is regularly ranked among New Jersey's wealthiest townships, and still looks, in patches, like the 18th-century farm country it grew out of.

Skillman Park

Somerset County's newest large park, built on the former North Princeton Developmental Center property the county bought in 2010. A 2.25-mile paved loop trail circles open fields and woods, with a rougher 3.4-mile nature trail, a two-acre leash-free dog run split for large and small dogs, chess tables at the trailheads, and a playground.

📍 Off County Route 601, Skillman · Free · Dawn–dusk

Trail Network9/11 Memorial

Montgomery Park

The township's own trail system off Harlingen Road — an Upper and Lower paved loop, an unpaved nature trail and the Back Brook Trail, linking on to Mill Pond Park. Picnic tables, a playground, seasonal restrooms, a native-plant arboretum and a 9/11 memorial round it out.

📍 Harlingen Road · Free · Three parking areas

HistorySince 1965

Van Harlingen Historical Society

Montgomery's own historical society, founded in 1965 to preserve the township's Dutch-settled farming heritage. It's restoring the 1752 Dirck Gulick House as its headquarters and archive, runs the one-room Bedensville Schoolhouse at Orchard Hill Elementary for school programs, and maintains the Montgomery Farm Museum.

📍 Belle Mead · Call ahead for tours

HistoryNational Register

Blawenburg Historic District

A 336-acre district along Great Road and County Route 518, settled around 1739 and largely built out by 1873 into the turnpike crossroads village that still stands — a general store, a blacksmith shop, and the 1830 Reformed Dutch Church of Blawenburg, which the congregation raised in just three days. Listed on the National Register of Historic Places since 1990.

📍 Great Road & CR 518, Blawenburg

Arts CenterCirca 1860

Montgomery Center for the Arts

A late Greek Revival farmhouse built around 1860 on what was once the 500-acre Van derVeer family farm, now the township's community arts center — exhibitions, classes and performances inside a genuinely old Montgomery building, known locally as the 1860 House.

📍 124 Montgomery Rd, Skillman

General Aviation

Princeton Airport

A small public-use general-aviation field actually sited in Montgomery Township, just west of Route 206 — flight training, charters and based aircraft, despite carrying a Princeton mailing address a few miles south.

📍 41 Airpark Rd (Princeton mailing address, Montgomery Twp)

Need to Know

🗺

Location: Somerset County, bordering Princeton to the south and Hillsborough, Hopewell and Franklin townships.

🏘

Villages: Skillman, Belle Mead and Blawenburg are unincorporated communities within the township, not separate towns — along with smaller Harlingen.

🚗

Getting there: Route 206 runs the length of the township north–south; County Routes 518 and 601 (Great Road) cross it and reach Blawenburg and Skillman.

🏪

No real downtown: Most everyday business — shops, restaurants, services — clusters along the Route 206 corridor rather than in the historic village centers.

💰

Character: Regularly ranked among New Jersey's wealthiest municipalities, with a median household income above $180,000.

Quick Tips

🚦

Blawenburg's historic crossroads is easy to miss at highway speed on CR 518 — worth the deliberate stop.

🚲

Skillman Park's paved 2.25-mile loop is the easiest trail in the township for strollers and bikes.

🍽

Nearly everything to eat is on Route 206, not inside the historic villages — plan around the corridor.

✈️

Princeton Airport (despite the name) is a Montgomery Township landmark, not a Princeton one.
