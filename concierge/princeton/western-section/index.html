Western Section

The historic west end of Princeton — Morven, Drumthwacket, the Battlefield and the Institute Woods, spread along Stockton and Mercer Streets. Big houses, long lawns and 250 years of history. No bars, no stages: this end of town is for daylight.

Landmarks & Museums

MuseumNational Historic Landmark

Morven Museum & Garden

Built in the 1750s by Richard Stockton, a signer of the Declaration of Independence, and later New Jersey's first governor's mansion. Now a museum with rotating exhibitions and gardens worth the visit on their own.

📍 Stockton St · Wed–Sun, 10 AM–4 PM · Garden dawn to dusk

Historic Home

Drumthwacket

The official residence of the Governor of New Jersey, a Greek Revival house set back behind a long lawn on Stockton Street. Open for scheduled public tours rather than drop-ins — check ahead.

📍 Stockton St · Tours by schedule

Battlefield1777

Princeton Battlefield State Park

Where Washington won the Battle of Princeton in January 1777, ten days after crossing the Delaware. Now open fields and the Thomas Clarke House — one of the quietest significant places in New Jersey.

📍 Mercer St · Grounds open daily

Institute Woods

Nearly 600 acres of woodland and meadow behind the Institute for Advanced Study, laced with easy trails and some of the best birding in central New Jersey. Open to the public, free, and remarkably under-walked.

📍 Behind the Institute for Advanced Study

Towpath

Delaware & Raritan Canal Towpath

The canal path runs along the edge of town — flat, shaded and uninterrupted, and the best running or cycling route in Princeton by a distance.

📍 Along the D&R Canal · Open daily
