Witherspoon-Jackson

Princeton's most historically significant African American community, just off Nassau Street and formally designated the town's 20th historic district in 2016. Residential, walkable, and carrying more of Princeton's actual history per block than anywhere else in town.

Princeton's 20th Historic District

Witherspoon-Jackson was formally designated Princeton's twentieth historic district in 2016 — recognition of a neighborhood that had been the center of the town's African American life for well over a century before anyone got around to putting a plaque on it.

📍 Witherspoon St corridor

ChurchLandmark

Witherspoon Street Presbyterian Church

One of the oldest Black congregations in New Jersey and the anchor of the neighborhood — a continuous musical and community tradition that predates every bar and stage in Princeton by a wide margin.

📍 Witherspoon St · Services & programs

History

The Name

The neighborhood takes its name from John Witherspoon — Princeton president, and the only active clergyman to sign the Declaration of Independence. The street named for him became the spine of a very different Princeton story than the one told on campus.

Hoagie Haven

Serving uniquely named, overstuffed hoagies and cheesesteaks since the 1970s. A genuine Princeton institution, no-frills by design, and the default answer to "where do students actually eat".
