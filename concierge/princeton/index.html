Princeton, NJ

A 280-year-old college town that runs two nightlife economies at once — an internationally programmed concert series on one side of Nassau Street, and a handful of stubborn bars playing local bands until 2 AM on the other.

Where to Eat

Dining

SeafoodSince 1999

Blue Point Grill

The town's benchmark for fish — a raw bar and a daily catch list sourced straight from the seafood market next door. Indoor and patio seating, and a following loyal enough to make walk-ins a gamble.

📍 258 Nassau St · $$$ · Dinner

American

Agricola

Communal tables, wood-fired cooking and plates built to share — roasted carrots, crispy trout salad, that register. Modern American with enough technique behind it to hold its own.

📍 Downtown Princeton · $$$ · Dinner

SandwichesInstitution

Hoagie Haven

Overstuffed hoagies and cheesesteaks under famously strange names, served since the 1970s. No-frills by design and the default answer to where students actually eat.

📍 Princeton · $ · Late

Steakhouse

Witherspoon Grill

Palmer Square's special-occasion room — steaks, a busy bar, and a patio that fills the moment the weather cooperates.

📍 Palmer Square · $$$ · Lunch & Dinner

French

Mistral

Small plates and a modern French-Mediterranean kitchen — the more adventurous of the square's sit-down options, and the one to pick when you want the meal to be the event.

📍 Palmer Square area · $$$ · Dinner

CoffeeInstitution

Small World Coffee

The town's coffee institution and the unofficial second office of half of Princeton. Roasts its own; the Saturday-morning line out the door is part of the deal.

📍 Nassau St · $ · Opens early

After Dark

Nightlife

Princeton's licensing rules do most of the sorting: three downtown bars can serve until 2 AM, and the residential-area bars have to close at midnight. Those three are where the town's nights actually happen.

TavernLandmark

The Alchemist & Barrister

The "Cheers of Princeton" — a landmark tavern in a historic building, with live local bands on Wednesday, Thursday and Sunday nights and a late license to see them through.

📍 28 Witherspoon St · Open until 2 AM

Brewpub

Triumph Brewing Company

A Nassau Street microbrewery with a genuinely mixed room — students, locals and the corporate crowd — and a rotating live-music calendar spread through the week.

📍 Nassau St · Open until 2 AM

Dive Bar

Ivy Inn

Pool tables, cheap drinks, friendly bartenders and music that turns up without much warning. The least polished of the late-license three, which is the whole appeal.

📍 Princeton · Open until 2 AM

On Stage & On Screen

Arts & Culture

Concert HallWorld-Class

Richardson Auditorium

Inside Alexander Hall, and home to Princeton University Concerts — a chamber series booking at international level, with the New Jersey Symphony playing its Princeton dates here too. Open to the public, not just the University.

📍 68 Nassau St · Season runs Sep–May

Theatre

McCarter Theatre Center

The town's big year-round house — live music alongside plays, ballet, circus and cabaret. Programs independently of the University series, so check both calendars.

📍 University Place · Year-round

Record StoreWorld-Class

Princeton Record Exchange

Buying and selling music and movies since 1980, with an inventory north of 100,000 LPs, CDs and DVDs that turns over constantly. Routinely named among the best independent record stores anywhere.

📍 Princeton · Open daily

Cinema

Princeton Garden Theatre

University-owned, run by Renew Theaters — first-run films of real artistic quality plus classics, foreign-language programming and Saturday kids' matinees. NJ.com named it the state's best movie theater.

📍 Nassau St · Daily screenings

MuseumLandmark

Morven Museum & Garden

Built in the 1750s by Richard Stockton, a signer of the Declaration, and later New Jersey's first governor's mansion. A National Historic Landmark with rotating exhibitions and gardens worth the trip alone.

📍 Stockton St · Wed–Sun, 10 AM–4 PM

MuseumFree

Princeton University Art Museum

One of the strongest university collections in the country, spanning antiquity to the contemporary — and free to visit.

📍 Princeton University campus

Outside

Parks & Trails

TrailsFree

Institute Woods

Nearly 600 acres of woodland and meadow behind the Institute for Advanced Study, with easy trails and some of the best birding in central New Jersey. Open to all, and remarkably under-walked.

📍 Behind the Institute for Advanced Study

Historic Park

Princeton Battlefield State Park

Where Washington won the Battle of Princeton in January 1777, ten days after crossing the Delaware. Open fields, the Thomas Clarke House, and one of the quietest significant places in New Jersey.

📍 Mercer St · Grounds open daily

Towpath

D&R Canal Towpath

Flat, shaded and uninterrupted along the edge of town — the best running or cycling route in Princeton by a wide margin.

📍 Along the D&R Canal · Open daily
