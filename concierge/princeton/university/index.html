University Campus

Founded in 1746 and sitting directly across Nassau Street from downtown, the campus is where Princeton keeps its serious music — an internationally programmed concert series, a year-round theatre, and a free art museum, all open to the town.

Richardson Auditorium

Inside Alexander Hall, and the home room for Princeton University Concerts — a chamber series that books at genuine international level. The New Jersey Symphony plays its Princeton dates here too. Tickets are open to the public, not just the University.

📍 68 Nassau St · Season runs Sep–May

Theatre

McCarter Theatre Center

The town's big year-round house — live music alongside plays, ballet, circus and cabaret. Programs entirely independently of the University concert series, so it is worth checking both calendars before you decide nothing is on.

📍 University Place · Year-round

Museum

Princeton University Art Museum

One of the strongest university collections in the country, spanning antiquity to the contemporary, and free to visit. Worth building an afternoon around.

The Princeton Campus

Founded in 1746 and one of the most walkable university campuses anywhere — Collegiate Gothic courtyards, Nassau Hall, and grounds that are open to anyone who wants to wander them. Self-guided is fine; the campus rewards aimlessness.

📍 South of Nassau St · Open daily

Green Space

Prospect Garden

A formal garden tucked behind Prospect House in the middle of campus. Small, quiet, and almost always empty — the best sit-down spot on the whole campus.
