New York

Queens, NY

From the Unisphere to the surf breaks off Beach 87th Street — Queens is New York's biggest borough by land and its most global by any measure: about 2.4 million people speaking some 150 languages across 109 square miles, the most linguistically diverse urban area on Earth. This guide starts with ten essential neighborhoods — the East River art towns, the Roosevelt Avenue food corridor, the World's Fair park where the US Open is played, and the city's own surf beach — with dozens more of the borough still to come.

Long Island City

Art on the Waterfront

Queens' East River gateway — a century of pure industry that has flipped, tower by tower, into the fastest-changing skyline in the city, with Midtown Manhattan one subway stop away. The art came first: P.S. 1 opened its contemporary art center inside a shuttered Romanesque public school in the 1970s, long before the glass condos, and the waterfront's restored rail gantries and 1940 Pepsi-Cola sign (landmarked in 2016) now preside over a chain of riverfront parks instead of barge terminals.

Museum · Free Through 2028
MoMA PS1
📍 22-25 Jackson Ave

The contemporary art center in a former public school became one of New York's largest free museums in January 2026 — admission is free to all for three years. Open Thu, Fri, Sun & Mon 12–6, Sat 10–6.

State Park
Gantry Plaza State Park
📍 Center Blvd, on the East River

Riverfront piers built around the preserved rail-barge gantries, with the landmarked 1940 Pepsi-Cola sign at the north end and a dead-on Midtown skyline view.

Waterfront Park
Hunter's Point South Park
📍 Center Blvd & Borden Ave

The newer southern stretch of the LIC waterfront — wetlands, a green oval, and a promenade that runs to the mouth of Newtown Creek.

Mexican
Casa Enrique
📍 5-48 49th Ave

Chef Cosme Aguilar's Chiapas-rooted kitchen held a Michelin star for nearly a decade (2014–2023) — the first Mexican restaurant in New York to earn one.

Cocktail Bar · Since 2009
Dutch Kills
📍 27-24 Jackson Ave

A wood-boothed craft-cocktail landmark named for the neighborhood's old creek — hand-cut ice and no shortcuts, nightly from 5 PM.

Free Arts Center
Culture Lab LIC
📍 5-25 46th Ave

A nonprofit gallery and performance campus with free outdoor "Live in The Lot" concerts on summer weekends and free Friday-night jazz at Culture Lab After Dark.

Bar · Live Music
LIC Bar
📍 45-58 Vernon Blvd

An old corner saloon with a brick back room and garden that books singer-songwriters and bands through the week.

Hotel · Rooftop Pool Club
Ravel Hotel
📍 8-08 Queens Plaza South

LIC's original boutique hotel, tucked under the Queensboro Bridge with the Profundo rooftop day club and pool looking straight at Midtown.

Astoria

Greek Queens & the Film Lot

Astoria has been Queens' great arrival neighborhood for a century — famously Greek, and now equally Middle Eastern, Balkan, Brazilian, and Mexican, block by block. It's also the borough's film town: the Museum of the Moving Image sits beside Kaufman Astoria Studios, a working movie lot since the silent era, and the neighborhood keeps two of the city's best summer institutions — the oldest beer garden in New York (1910) and the biggest WPA-era pool (1936).

Museum · Free Thursdays
Museum of the Moving Image
📍 36-01 35th Ave

Film, TV and digital media in all their machinery — home of the Jim Henson Exhibition's forty-plus puppets. Free Thursdays 2–6 PM; closed Mon–Wed.

Sculpture Park · Free · Est. 1986
Socrates Sculpture Park
📍 32-01 Vernon Blvd

An illegal dump turned outdoor museum, marking its 40th anniversary in 2026 with the "Artists Choose Artists" fellowship exhibition. Free, 9 AM to sunset, 365 days.

Park & Pool · Since 1936
Astoria Park & Pool
📍 Between the Triborough & Hell Gate bridges

Lawns under two bridges on the East River, plus the largest pool in New York City — 330 feet long, the first of Robert Moses's giant 1936 WPA pools.

Beer Garden · Since 1910
Bohemian Hall & Beer Garden
📍 29-19 24th Ave

New York's oldest beer garden, built by the Bohemian Citizens' Benevolent Society and pouring Czech lagers under the trees for over a century.

Greek Seafood
Taverna Kyclades
📍 36-01 Ditmars Blvd

The no-reservations Ditmars institution for grilled octopus and whole fish — the wait on the sidewalk is part of the ritual.

Sunnyside

The Garden Suburb

Behind the neon arch over Queens Boulevard hides a piece of planning history: Sunnyside Gardens (1924–28), the first planned garden community in the United States, designed by Clarence Stein and Henry Wright around shared interior courtyards — critic Lewis Mumford lived here for years and championed it. The rest of the neighborhood is one of Queens' most easygoing dining strips, where Irish pubs share blocks with Colombian, Bolivian, Nepali and Romanian kitchens along Queens Boulevard and Skillman Avenue.

Historic District · 1924–28
Sunnyside Gardens
📍 Between Skillman & 39th Aves, 43rd–52nd Sts

Seventy-seven acres of brick rowhouses around common gardens — America's first garden community, made a city historic district in 2007.

Pizzeria
Philomena's
📍 41-16 Queens Blvd

A family-run pizzeria that turns out some of the best pies in Queens — square and round, under the 7 train.

Bolivian
Bolivian Llama Party
📍 44-14 48th Ave

The brothers-run spot that made salteñas — Bolivia's soupy, sweet-savory hand pies — a New York obsession.

Italian
SoleLuna
📍 40-01 Queens Blvd

A sun-soaked neighborhood Italian room on the Boulevard — handmade pastas and dinner specials that keep the regulars regular.

Irish Grocer & Deli
Butcher Block
📍 43-46 Queens Blvd

The Irish import grocery and butcher counter that has kept Sunnyside in rashers, bangers and brown bread for decades.

Jackson Heights

The World on 74th Street

Built between 1914 and 1939 as the nation's first planned garden-apartment cooperative community — its leafy interior-courtyard blocks were designated a historic district in 1993 — Jackson Heights is now routinely cited among the most diverse neighborhoods on Earth. Little India has anchored 74th Street since the 1970s, Diversity Plaza fills the block of 37th Road by the subway, and you can hear a dozen languages between one avenue and the next.

Historic District · Designated 1993
Jackson Heights Historic District
📍 Roughly 76th–88th Sts, 37th Ave to Roosevelt

Garden apartments built around private interior parks — walk 80th to 88th Streets between 37th and 35th Avenues for the showpiece blocks.

Public Plaza
Diversity Plaza
📍 37th Rd, between 73rd & 74th Sts

The pedestrianized block by the Roosevelt Ave subway hub — momo carts, chai, and the neighborhood's living room in the open air.

Little India · Since the 1970s
74th Street
📍 74th St, 37th Ave to Roosevelt Ave

The historic South Asian shopping street — sari houses, gold jewelers, sweet shops and grocers that have drawn the region's diaspora for fifty years.

Colombian · Street-Cart Legend
Arepa Lady
📍 77-15A 37th Ave

María Piedad Cano's legendary Roosevelt Avenue arepa cart grew into this family-run storefront — the arepa de choclo with cheese is the order.

Diner & Ice Cream · Brand Est. 1897
Jahn's
📍 81-04 37th Ave

The last surviving Jahn's ice cream parlor anywhere — vintage booths, diner breakfasts, and the group-size "Kitchen Sink" sundae.

Elmhurst

Newtown Since 1652

Elmhurst began as Newtown, one of Queens' original English towns, settled in 1652 — Old St. James Church (1735), the borough's oldest surviving building of its kind, still stands just off Broadway. Today it's one of the city's dining capitals: a busy Chinatown, a Little Thailand, and Vietnamese, Indonesian and Latin American kitchens packed around the Broadway–Roosevelt corridor, with a Latin jazz stage hiding on a side street.

Landmark · Built 1735
Old St. James Church
📍 86-02 Broadway

The Church of England's colonial mission church at Newtown — the city's second-oldest religious building, landmarked in 2017.

Thai · Since 2008
Ayada Thai
📍 77-08 Woodside Ave

The family-run standard-bearer of Elmhurst's Thai row — order the som tum and anything with the word "spicy" taken seriously.

Isan Thai
Zaab Zaab
📍 76-04 Woodside Ave

Fiery northeastern-Thai cooking — larb, raw-shrimp salads and grilled seafood — that earned a spot in the Michelin Guide.

Northern Chinese
Lao Bei Fang Dumpling House
📍 83-05 Broadway

Pan-fried dumplings with shattering bottoms and hand-pulled noodle soups, made in view and priced like a secret.

Live Music · Nightly
Terraza 7
📍 40-19 Gleane St, at the Jackson Heights border

A two-level Latin jazz and world-music room with performances almost every night on its suspended stage — open daily 4 PM to 4 AM.

Corona & Flushing Meadows

Satchmo, Ices & the Open

Louis Armstrong could have lived anywhere on Earth; he chose a modest house on 107th Street in Corona from 1943 until his death in 1971, and it's now one of the country's great house museums. The old Italian Corona of lemon ices and latticini shares the neighborhood with one of the city's largest Latin American communities — and next door spreads Flushing Meadows Corona Park, built for the 1939 and 1964 World's Fairs, where the Unisphere, two major museums, and the US Open all live.

House Museum
Louis Armstrong House Museum
📍 34-56 107th St

Satchmo's preserved home, toured hourly Thu–Sat — with the Armstrong Center's "Here to Stay" exhibition and 75-seat jazz room across the street.

Italian Ices · Since 1944
The Lemon Ice King of Corona
📍 52-02 108th St

The Benfaremo family's walk-up window has scooped real-fruit ices for eighty years — and stars in the King of Queens opening credits. Daily 12–8.

Italian Deli
Leo's Latticini (Mama's of Corona)
📍 46-02 104th St

Fresh mozzarella pulled in back and heroes built with it up front — a Corona family institution. Tue–Sat 10–4.

World's Fair Icon · 1964
The Unisphere
📍 Flushing Meadows Corona Park

The twelve-story stainless-steel globe built for the 1964–65 World's Fair, ringed by fountains — the symbol of Queens.

Museum · The Panorama
Queens Museum
📍 In the park, beside the Unisphere

Home of the Panorama of the City of New York — the astonishing block-by-block scale model of all five boroughs made for the 1964 Fair. Wed–Sun 11–5.

Science Museum
New York Hall of Science
📍 47-01 111th St

The hands-on science center in the Fair's Great Hall — and host field for the Queens Night Market's food stalls on Saturday nights in season.

Tennis · Aug 23 – Sep 13, 2026
US Open at the Billie Jean King National Tennis Center
📍 Flushing Meadows Corona Park

The year's last Grand Slam, in the park since 1978 — free-admission Fan Week runs Aug 23–29, and the main draw plays Aug 30 to Sep 13 under Arthur Ashe Stadium's roof.

Flushing

Main Street Eats

The 7 train's last stop lands in one of the largest Chinese-speaking downtowns outside Asia — Main Street's malls, food courts and bakeries turn over regional Chinese cooking at a pace no other New York neighborhood matches. It's also colonial Queens: the 1694 Friends Meeting House on Northern Boulevard is New York City's oldest house of worship in continuous use, in the town where the 1657 Flushing Remonstrance staked an early American claim to religious liberty.

Food Court
New World Mall Food Court
📍 136-20 Roosevelt Ave, lower level

The big one — dozens of stalls of hand-pulled noodles, hot pot, dumplings and bubble tea under one roof, daily 10 AM–10 PM.

Soup Dumplings · Since 2006
Nan Xiang Xiao Long Bao
📍 39-16 Prince St

The xiao long bao destination of Flushing — a Michelin Guide pick for nine straight years, now in a bigger Prince Street home.

Historic Site · Built 1694
Friends Quaker Meeting House
📍 137-16 Northern Blvd

New York City's oldest house of worship in continuous use — Quakers still meet Sundays, with free tours after worship, 12–12:30.

Arts Center · Jazz
Flushing Town Hall
📍 137-35 Northern Blvd

The borough's global-arts presenter — jazz, world music and exhibitions year-round, including monthly all-comers jazz jams.

Garden
Queens Botanical Garden
📍 43-50 Main St

Thirty-nine acres off Main Street — $6 in season (free Wed 3–6 and Sun 9–11), and free altogether from mid-December through March. Closed Mondays.

Forest Hills

Tennis, Tudors & Encores

For half a century this was the center of American tennis: the West Side Tennis Club's 1923 horseshoe stadium hosted the U.S. National Championships and US Open from 1924 through 1977, crowning champions from Althea Gibson to Arthur Ashe. The Beatles played their first stadium concerts here in August 1964 — and after decades of silence the stadium is again one of the city's great concert venues, ringed by the storybook Tudor blocks of Forest Hills Gardens.

Concert Stadium · Since 1923
Forest Hills Stadium
📍 1 Tennis Pl

The 13,000-seat tennis horseshoe where Dylan, the Stones and the Beatles played — its 2026 season runs June 6 to October 10, from Zac Brown Band to David Byrne and Erykah Badu.

Planned Enclave · Est. 1909
Station Square & Forest Hills Gardens
📍 At the LIRR station, off Continental Ave

A brick-arcaded Tudor square straight out of an English village, gateway to the private-street garden suburb beyond — best seen on foot.

Historic Club
West Side Tennis Club
📍 1 Tennis Pl

The century-plus-old private club whose grounds hold the stadium — grass courts and all; the US Open's home before it moved to Flushing Meadows in 1978.

Soda Fountain · Site Since 1925
Eddie's Sweet Shop
📍 105-29 Metropolitan Ave

A working time capsule of a soda fountain — marble counter, wooden booths, homemade ice cream and real whipped cream, run as Eddie's since 1967.

Pizzeria · Since 1993
Nick's Pizza
📍 108-26 Ascan Ave

Thin, blistered, no-slices pies a block from Austin Street — a Forest Hills fixture for three decades.

Ridgewood

Brick Rows & Late Nights

A turn-of-the-century brick-rowhouse district on the Brooklyn border, built by German brewers and knitters — the NRHP-listed Stockholm Street block still shows the mold: matched rows on a brick-paved street. The old world holds on at Gottscheer Hall (1924), clubhouse of the Gottschee German-speaking community, while a newer Ridgewood has made the neighborhood one of the city's best places to hear music after midnight — and its oldest Dutch stone house (1709) still stands on Flushing Avenue.

Hall & Bar · Since 1924
Gottscheer Hall
📍 657 Fairview Ave

The Gottscheer community's century-old hall — a wood-paneled bar up front, a ballroom in back, and bands, parties and trivia nights on the calendar.

Club · Indoor-Outdoor
Nowadays
📍 56-06 Cooper Ave

A dance club with a listening-room conscience and a big backyard — daytime parties in summer, marathon sets year-round.

Venue & Bar
TV Eye
📍 1647 Weirfield St

Rock club, bar and discotheque from Bowery Ballroom and WFMU alumni — the borough's home for touring underground bands.

Wood-Fired · Bar & Grill
Rolo's
📍 853 Onderdonk Ave

A Michelin Guide-listed neighborhood grill where everything passes through the wood oven — with a bakery counter by day.

Historic District
Stockholm Street Historic District
📍 Stockholm St, between Onderdonk & Woodward

An intact block of circa-1900 brick rowhouses on a brick-paved street — the picture of workers' Ridgewood, on the National Register.

Dutch Colonial · 1709
Vander Ende-Onderdonk House
📍 1820 Flushing Ave

The oldest Dutch Colonial stone house in New York City, on a site farmed since 1661 — open for tours and public events.

Rockaway Beach

The City's Surf Town

An hour on the A train or a ferry ride from Manhattan lands you at New York's only surf town — the ocean-facing strip of the Rockaway peninsula where the city's designated surfing beaches break off Beach 87th–92nd Streets. The 2026 swim season runs through September 13, and the boardwalk's concession culture is a destination in itself. Just west of the neighborhood, Jacob Riis Park — "The People's Beach," federal parkland in Gateway National Recreation Area — reopened its Art Deco bathhouse in summer 2026 after fifty boarded-up years.

Beach & Boardwalk · Thru Sep 13
Rockaway Beach & Boardwalk
📍 Concessions at Beach 86th & Beach 97th Sts

The city's ocean beach, rebuilt boardwalk and all — lifeguarded swimming through September 13, surfers in the water year-round.

Beach Club & Tacos · Seasonal
Rockaway Beach Surf Club
📍 302 Beach 87th St

The surf-shack bar and event space that anchors the 87th Street scene, home of Tacoway Beach's beer-battered fish tacos — open daily in season, mid-May through October.

Boardwalk Burgers · Seasonal
Rippers
📍 On the boardwalk at Beach 86th St

Smash burgers, frozen drinks and DJs right on the boardwalk — the definitive Rockaway summer lunch, weather permitting.

Bar · Frozen Piña Coladas
Connolly's
📍 155 Beach 95th St

A cash-bar beach dive pouring its famous frozen piña coladas in paper cups, Memorial Day through Labor Day.

Next Door · Federal Parkland
Jacob Riis Park
📍 West of Beach 116th St — Gateway National Recreation Area

"The People's Beach" on national parkland just west of the neighborhood — its restored 1930s bathhouse reopened in 2026 as the Rockaway Ocean Club's courtyard of shops and concessions.
