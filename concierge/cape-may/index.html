Cape May County · Southern Jersey Shore

Cape May

The nation's oldest seaside resort, at the southernmost tip of New Jersey — and the only city in the country wholly designated a National Historic Landmark. Roughly 600 Victorian buildings, most rebuilt after a five-day fire tore through downtown in 1878, fill a 2.5-square-mile city that runs on the pedestrian Washington Street Mall by day and a nearly two-mile beachfront promenade at every hour. First drawing Philadelphia vacationers in the 1700s, it was still pulling presidents a century later — and the gingerbread trim never went out of style.

Attractions

Pedestrian MallDowntown

Washington Street Mall

A three-block pedestrian promenade — the southernmost stretch of Washington Street, from Ocean Street to Perry Street — lined with boutiques, fudge shops, galleries and sidewalk cafés. The commercial heart of the historic downtown since the street was closed to cars in 1971.

📍 Washington St, Ocean St to Perry St · Open-air, year-round

MuseumBuilt 1879

Emlen Physick Estate

An 18-room Stick Style mansion designed by architect Frank Furness for Dr. Emlen Physick Jr., now the centerpiece house-museum of Cape May MAC's guided tours — period rooms, a carriage house and grounds that anchor the city's Victorian identity.

📍 1048 Washington St · Guided tours, Cape May MAC

Since 1816National Historic Landmark

Congress Hall

America's first seaside resort hotel — a wooden boarding house rebuilt in brick after the 1878 fire, and a Cape May landmark ever since. Occupies its own block between Beach Avenue and Congress Place, with the Blue Pig Tavern and the Boiler Room music club inside.

📍 251 Beach Ave / 200 Congress Pl

~2 MilesFree

The Promenade & Beach

A paved seawall promenade runs parallel to Beach Avenue for nearly two miles, replacing the wooden boardwalk the 1962 Ash Wednesday nor'easter destroyed. Cape May was the first Jersey Shore resort to build a boardwalk at all, back in the late 1860s. Beach tags are required in season.

📍 Beach Ave, city-wide · Beach badges required Memorial Day–Labor Day

Rebuilt 2012Beachfront

Cape May Convention Hall

A 20,000-square-foot beachfront hall for concerts, seminars and events, on the site of the original 1918 Convention Hall lost in the same 1962 storm that took the boardwalk. Reopened in 2012 with concerts and community events on the sand.

📍 714 Beach Ave · Seasonal events calendar

Harbor Cruises

Whale & Dolphin Watching

Two boats sail out of Cape May Harbor for the Delaware Bay and Atlantic — the Cape May Whale Watcher from Miss Chris Marina, running since 1993, and the Cape May Whale Watch & Research Center's American Star. Bottlenose dolphins are a near-guaranteed sight; whales are seasonal.

📍 Miss Chris Marina, Cape May Harbor · Seasonal, March–December

Dining & Nightlife

New AmericanVirginia Hotel

The Ebbitt Room

Seasonal tasting menus built around Cape May scallops and produce from Beach Plum Farm, in the Virginia Hotel's elegant dining room with live piano most nights. One of the city's standing fine-dining rooms.

📍 25 Jackson St · Reservations recommended

Breakfast · DinnerCape May Institution

The Mad Batter

A longtime Cape May favorite for breakfast, lunch and dinner in a Victorian guesthouse setting with porch seating — famous locally for its breakfasts as much as its dinner menu.

📍 19 Jackson St

Farm-to-TableInside Congress Hall

Blue Pig Tavern

Congress Hall's all-day tavern — breakfast, brunch, lunch and dinner built largely on ingredients from Beach Plum Farm three miles away, served in a corner room of the historic hotel.

📍 251 Beach Ave, Congress Hall

American · Wine BarSince 1979

Washington Inn & Wine Bar

An 1840 estate turned six-room dining house with the city's deepest wine list, fireside tables and a summer patio — a fixture of Cape May's upscale dinner scene for decades.

📍 801 Washington St · Complimentary valet

Contemporary AmericanBeach Ave

Peter Shields Inn & Restaurant

Award-winning seasonal dining in a beachfront inn, with outdoor seating and a fireplace dining room — a quieter, less touristy fine-dining option on the beach side of town.

📍 1301 Beach Ave

Dockside SeafoodFamily-Owned

The Lobster House

A Cape May Harbor institution at Fisherman's Wharf — a sit-down dining room, a raw bar, and a retail fish market, all family-run and dockside since the fleet still comes in a few steps away. No reservations.

📍 906 Schellengers Landing Rd, Fisherman's Wharf

Surfer BarSince the '70s

Rusty Nail

Cape May's iconic surfer bar — "the Nail" to locals — wrapped in the semicircle of the Beach Shack motel, with indoor and outdoor stages for regular live acts through the season. No reservations.

📍 205 Beach Ave

Need to Know

🗺

Location: Cape May County, at the very southern tip of New Jersey — a distinct city, separate from neighboring West Cape May and Cape May Point boroughs.

🏖

Beach badges: Required Memorial Day–Labor Day on the guarded beaches along the promenade.

🚗

Getting there: The end of the Garden State Parkway — Exit 0, the source of the jazz festival's name. About 2.5 hours from Philadelphia or NYC.

⛴

Ferry: The Cape May–Lewes Ferry to Delaware departs from North Cape May, in neighboring Lower Township — a short drive, not inside city limits.

🗼

Lighthouse: The Cape May Lighthouse and hawk-watch platform are about two miles away in Cape May Point State Park (Lower Township / Cape May Point) — a worthwhile side trip, but a separate town from the city itself.

📅

Peak season: Memorial Day–Labor Day, plus a real second season around the Victorian Week and Christmas events each winter.

Quick Tips

🚲

The city is flat and 2.5 square miles — most visitors leave the car parked and walk or bike everywhere.

🍽

Book fine-dining rooms like the Ebbitt Room or Peter Shields Inn ahead in summer; the Lobster House and Rusty Nail don't take reservations.

🎨

Every building along Washington Street Mall and the surrounding blocks is part of the same National Historic Landmark district — worth slowing down for.

🐬

Dolphin sightings on the harbor cruises are close to guaranteed; whales are seasonal and never promised.
