New York

Brooklyn, NY

Kings County was its own city until 1898, and it never quite stopped acting like one — about 2.7 million people across 70 square miles, more than any other borough, enough that an independent Brooklyn would be the fourth-largest city in America. This guide starts with ten essential neighborhoods: the East River waterfront from Greenpoint to DUMBO, the brownstone belt from Brooklyn Heights to Park Slope, the museums on the park's flank, Bushwick's murals, and the boardwalk empire at Coney Island — with dozens more of the borough still to come.

Williamsburg

Bedford Ave & the Bridge

The neighborhood that rewrote what "Brooklyn" means to the rest of the world — a waterfront of former refineries and breweries turned into parks, food markets and venues, with the oldest steakhouse in the city still holding down Broadway under the bridge (since 1887). It's a neighborhood in visible motion right now: Brooklyn Brewery pours its last beers on North 11th Street on August 29 before moving a few blocks north, and the Music Hall of Williamsburg is playing out its final year.

Food Market · Saturdays thru October
Smorgasburg Williamsburg
📍 Marsha P. Johnson State Park, 90 Kent Ave

The giant open-air food market on the East River — 70-plus vendors every Saturday 11 AM–6 PM, April through October, with the Manhattan skyline for a backdrop. Free to enter.

Last Pours · Aug 29
Brooklyn Brewery
📍 79 N 11th St

The brewery that helped launch craft beer in New York closes its longtime North 11th Street home on August 29, 2026 — a farewell party with fresh beer and live music — before reopening at 1 Wythe Ave, across the Greenpoint line, this fall.

Steakhouse · Est. 1887
Peter Luger Steak House
📍 178 Broadway

The wood-paneled porterhouse temple under the Williamsburg Bridge, serving since 1887 — still the standard every New York steakhouse gets measured against.

Venue · Final Year
Music Hall of Williamsburg
📍 66 N 6th St

The Bowery Presents club that anchored the neighborhood's indie era closes at the end of 2026 after losing its lease — but the calendar runs full through December. Catch it while you can.

Waterfront Park
Domino Park
📍 300 Kent Ave, at the Williamsburg Bridge

Six acres of public waterfront built on the old Domino Sugar Refinery site — a tribute to the generations of Domino workers, with salvaged refinery machinery standing along the esplanade.

New Music
National Sawdust
📍 80 N 6th St

A nonprofit incubator for new and experimental music in a jewel-box room with a state-of-the-art Meyer spatial sound system — composers and premieres you won't hear anywhere else.

Indie Room · 330 Cap
Baby's All Right
📍 146 Broadway

The Southside's essential small stage — a 330-capacity room and bar where rising acts of every genre land almost nightly.

Park & WPA Pool
McCarren Park & Pool
📍 Pool at 776 Lorimer St

The big shared backyard on the Greenpoint line — ballfields, a track, and the huge McCarren Pool, open free each summer from late June through early September.

Greenpoint

Little Poland on the East River

Brooklyn's northernmost neighborhood is still the heart of Polish New York — pierogi and paczki along Manhattan Avenue, a 1914 Polish National Home that doubles as one of the city's best rock rooms — with a working-waterfront edge that has softened into parks and ferry stops. The next chapter is already arriving: Brooklyn Brewery's new home at 1 Wythe Ave, in the Greenpoint-Williamsburg industrial zone, opens this fall.

Waterfront Park
WNYC Transmitter Park
📍 Greenpoint Ave at the East River

Six and a half acres where WNYC's old radio towers once stood — lawns, gardens and a fishing pier pointed straight at Midtown.

Venue · Polish National Home
Warsaw
📍 261 Driggs Ave

The ballroom of the 1914 Polish National Home has hosted punk and indie shows since 2001 — a 1,000-capacity hall where you can eat pierogi and kielbasa while the band plays.

Polish Tavern
Karczma
📍 136 Greenpoint Ave

A wood-beamed country tavern that has packed them in for well over a decade — platters of kielbasa, hunter's stew and potato pancakes at gentle prices.

Donut Counter · Since 1953
Peter Pan Donut & Pastry Shop
📍 727 Manhattan Ave

The Polish-run counter that has fried Greenpoint's donuts since the early 1950s — old booths, paper hats, and a line out the door on weekends.

Opening This Fall
Brooklyn Brewery's Next Home
📍 1 Wythe Ave

The brewery's new headquarters — nearly four times the size of the old one — lands on the Greenpoint side of the line, with brewery tours and guided tastings set to return when it opens this fall.

DUMBO

Between the Bridges

Down Under the Manhattan Bridge Overpass: Belgian-block streets and Civil War-era warehouses wedged between the two great East River bridges, now the borough's postcard — the Washington Street view of the Manhattan Bridge arch is one of the most photographed corners in New York. The old ferry landing at Old Fulton Street holds a genuine pizza dynasty, and the warehouses hold a carousel, a food hall and one of the country's most adventurous theaters.

The Waterfront
Brooklyn Bridge Park — Empire Fulton Ferry
📍 Water St between the bridges

The DUMBO stretch of the 85-acre park that runs 1.3 miles along six piers of formerly abandoned shoreline — this is the lawn-under-the-bridge section, with the skyline dead ahead.

Landmark Carousel
Jane's Carousel
📍 Dock St & Water St, in the park

A lovingly restored antique carousel turning in a glass pavilion right on the river — summer hours Wed–Mon 11 AM–7 PM, winter Thu–Sun 11–6, closed Tuesdays.

Food Hall · Rooftop
Time Out Market New York
📍 55 Water St, in Empire Stores

Twenty-four thousand square feet of hand-picked New York eateries and three bars inside the Empire Stores warehouse, with a fifth-floor rooftop staring at both bridges. Open daily from 8 AM.

Theater · Fall Season
St. Ann's Warehouse
📍 45 Water St

The adventurous stage in an 1860s tobacco warehouse opens its fall 2026 season with Daniel Fish's Kramer/Fauci (Sep 27–Oct 24), Emma Rice's Tristan & Yseult and Saigon — plus 50 life-size buffalo puppets parading through Downtown Brooklyn Sep 19–20.

Pizza · The Return
Juliana's
📍 19 Old Fulton St

Patsy Grimaldi came out of retirement in 2012 to reclaim his original address under the bridge, naming the new place for his mother — coal-oven pies daily, 11:30 AM on.

Pizza · The Original Name
Grimaldi's
📍 1 Front St

The pizzeria Patsy founded in 1990 and sold in the '90s now bakes next door at 1 Front Street — so the corner of Old Fulton holds both halves of Brooklyn's great pizza rivalry.

Brooklyn Heights

America's First Suburb

Rows of Greek Revival and brownstone streets above the harbor, commuting to Wall Street by ferry since the early 1800s — with the whole show summed up from the Promenade, the walkway cantilevered over the expressway with the Lower Manhattan skyline, the harbor and the Statue of Liberty laid out in one panorama. Montague Street is the neighborhood's main street, and the borough's own history museum lives in a landmark hall on Pierrepont.

The View
Brooklyn Heights Promenade
📍 Enter at Montague, Pierrepont, Clark or Pineapple Sts

An 1,826-foot esplanade cantilevered over the BQE — the unobstructed panorama of Lower Manhattan, the bridges, the harbor and the Statue of Liberty that everyone means when they say "the view from Brooklyn."

Museum & Library · Wed–Sun
Center for Brooklyn History
📍 128 Pierrepont St

The borough's attic — Brooklyn Public Library's history center in the landmark former Long Island Historical Society hall, freshly renovated and open Wed–Sun 12–5.

Landmark · Est. 1847
Plymouth Church
📍 57 Orange St

Henry Ward Beecher's abolitionist congregation — a National Historic Landmark and Underground Railroad station, with history tours most Sundays at 12:30 after worship.

Grocer · On Atlantic Since 1948
Sahadi's
📍 187 Atlantic Ave

The Middle Eastern grocery that started in Manhattan in 1895 and has anchored Atlantic Avenue since 1948 — barrels of olives, roasted nuts and spices, now on the state's Historic Business Preservation Registry.

The Main Street
Montague Street
📍 Montague St, Promenade to Court St

The Heights' restaurant row and shopping street, running from the Promenade's southern gate past the Gothic Revival towers of St. Ann & the Holy Trinity.

Museum · In a Real Station
New York Transit Museum
📍 99 Schermerhorn St

Vintage subway cars lined up on real tracks in a decommissioned station — the city's transit story told underground, on the Heights' side of the Downtown line.

Downtown Brooklyn

Fulton & Flatbush

The borough's civic and commercial core — Borough Hall presiding over the courts and the Fulton Street shopping spine, with a food hall in the basement of a shopping tower and the original Junior's holding the corner of Flatbush and DeKalb. Two neighbors sit just over the district lines: Barclays Center at Atlantic & Flatbush (listed under Park Slope, where its block actually falls) and the Brooklyn Paramount on the Fort Greene side of Flatbush Avenue Extension.

Food Hall · 40 Vendors
Dekalb Market Hall
📍 445 Albee Square West, below City Point

Sixty thousand square feet of New York food under one roof — about forty vendors' worth of everything, in the basement of the City Point complex.

The Cheesecake
Junior's
📍 386 Flatbush Ave Ext, at DeKalb Ave

The original — the orange-boothed Brooklyn institution whose New York-style cheesecake made the corner of Flatbush and DeKalb famous.

Civic Landmark
Brooklyn Borough Hall
📍 209 Joralemon St

The old City of Brooklyn's city hall, still the borough's front porch — and the hub of the Brooklyn Book Festival's stages each September.

Book Fest · Sep 20–28
Brooklyn Book Festival
📍 Festival Day Sep 27 around Borough Hall

One of the country's big free literary festivals — nine days of author events citywide, with Festival Day and its literary marketplace filling eight indoor and outdoor stages around Borough Hall on September 27.

Dine-In Cinema
Alamo Drafthouse — City Point
📍 445 Albee Square West, 4th & 5th floors

The movie-obsessive dine-in cinema atop City Point — recliners with built-in tables, strict no-talking rules, and a repertory calendar alongside first runs.

Fort Greene

The Park & the Academy

Brownstone blocks around a Revolutionary hilltop park, with the Brooklyn Academy of Music — BAM — as the neighborhood's cultural engine on Lafayette Avenue. The park's summit carries the Prison Ship Martyrs' Monument, a 149-foot Doric column over a crypt for the thousands of American prisoners who died on British ships in Wallabout Bay; the streets below carry one of the borough's best Saturday markets and its flagship independent bookstore.

Park & Monument
Fort Greene Park
📍 Between Myrtle & DeKalb Aves

The hilltop park crowned by Stanford White's 1908 Prison Ship Martyrs' Monument — a 149-foot granite column honoring the 11,500-plus patriots who died on the British prison ships anchored offshore.

Performing Arts · Next Wave
BAM — Brooklyn Academy of Music
📍 Peter Jay Sharp Building, 30 Lafayette Ave

America's great avant-garde house. The fall 2026 Next Wave festival builds toward DanceAfrica's 50th anniversary — and the Philip Glass Ensemble plays Einstein on the Beach 50th-anniversary concerts Nov 19–21.

Concert Palace
Brooklyn Paramount
📍 385 Flatbush Ave Ext

The gilded Paramount movie palace reborn as a full-time concert hall on the Fort Greene side of Flatbush Ave Extension — Peter Hook & The Light (Sep 5), Lucki (Sep 8–9) and Polyphia (Oct 5) this fall.

Bookstore
Greenlight Bookstore
📍 686 Fulton St

Fort Greene's flagship independent — a deep events calendar of author nights, plus story time across the street at the park's Saturday Greenmarket.

Cuban · Seasonal Courtyard
Habana Outpost
📍 757 Fulton St

The corner Cuban courtyard party — grilled corn and frozen mojitos in season, back in the swing after its long pandemic pause.

Market · Saturdays Year-Round
Fort Greene Park Greenmarket
📍 Washington Park, DeKalb to Willoughby

GrowNYC's market along the park's chestnut-shaded eastern edge — farmstands, compost and clothing collection every Saturday, 8 AM–4 PM, all year.

Park Slope

Brownstones to the Plaza

The brownstone neighborhood par excellence, sloping from the Gowanus flats up to Prospect Park West — Fifth and Seventh Avenues carry the restaurants and bars, and the city's shape for the neighborhood runs north to Atlantic Avenue and across the Grand Army Plaza oval, which is why the arena and the arch both belong to this section. The Revolutionary War's biggest early battle swept across these blocks in August 1776, and its most storied site still stands in Washington Park.

Arena · At Atlantic & Flatbush
Barclays Center
📍 620 Atlantic Ave

The borough's arena — Nets basketball (home opener Oct 21 vs. Charlotte), Liberty games, and a fall of big rooms: Robyn (Sep 4 & 10), Charli xcx (Sep 14–15), Phoebe Bridgers (Sep 24–26), Weezer (Sep 30).

Market · Saturdays Year-Round
Grand Army Plaza Greenmarket
📍 NW plaza entrance to Prospect Park

Greenmarket's flagship Brooklyn market since 1989 and the program's second largest — dozens of regional farmstands every Saturday 8 AM–4 PM, all year.

Monument · Restored 2025
Soldiers' & Sailors' Memorial Arch
📍 Grand Army Plaza

Brooklyn's triumphal arch, dedicated 1892 to the Union forces with Frederick MacMonnies' bronzes on John Duncan's granite — fresh from an $8.9M restoration completed in June 2025.

Bar · Bocce · Basement Stage
Union Hall
📍 702 Union St

A library-with-fireplaces bar with two indoor bocce courts upstairs and a downstairs room for comedy and bands — famous for secret shows by the likes of St. Vincent and Fleet Foxes.

Venetian · Since 1998
al di là Trattoria
📍 248 Fifth Ave

The corner Venetian trattoria that has anchored Fifth Avenue dining since 1998 — braises, handmade pasta, and a room that has barely needed to change.

World Music · Nightly
Barbès
📍 376 9th St

A little French-owned bar with live music nightly in the back room — Peruvian cumbia, Guinean jazz, Venezuelan harp, Balkan brass — one of the great listening rooms in the city.

Battle of Brooklyn · 1776
The Old Stone House
📍 336 3rd St, in Washington Park

A 1933 rebuilding of the 1699 Vechte-Cortelyou farmhouse where the Maryland 400 made their sacrificial stand in the Battle of Brooklyn (Aug 27, 1776) — now a small museum and community hall.

Prospect Heights

Museums on the Meadow

A compact brownstone neighborhood with an outsize cultural flank: the Brooklyn Museum, the Botanic Garden, the Central Library and Prospect Park itself all line its edges along Eastern Parkway and Flatbush — this guide draws the neighborhood and Olmsted & Vaux's 526-acre park as one piece. Vanderbilt Avenue is the restaurant spine, car-free on summer Saturdays, and Carnival takes over Eastern Parkway every Labor Day.

Museum · First Saturdays
Brooklyn Museum
📍 200 Eastern Parkway

New York's second-largest museum — 560,000 square feet of McKim, Mead & White Beaux-Arts holding half a million objects. Open Wed–Sun 11–6 (Thu to 10), with free First Saturday nights 5–11 in select months — next on Oct 3; there's no September edition.

Garden · Closed Mondays
Brooklyn Botanic Garden
📍 990 Washington Ave

The beloved garden beside the museum, laid out by the Olmsted Brothers' firm — $22 adults, under-12s free, with early 8 AM openings in the warm months.

The Park · 526 Acres
Prospect Park
📍 Main entrance at Grand Army Plaza

Olmsted and Vaux's 526-acre landscape, with Grand Army Plaza laid out in 1867 as its grand formal entrance — the Long Meadow, the Ravine and the lake unfold from there.

Library · Landmark
Central Library, Brooklyn Public Library
📍 10 Grand Army Plaza

The system's Art Deco flagship — a 50-foot gilded entry portico set into a concave limestone façade that mirrors the plaza's ellipse. A city landmark since 1997.

Open Street · Sat thru September
Vanderbilt Avenue Open Street
📍 Vanderbilt Ave, Atlantic Ave to Grand Army Plaza

The neighborhood's dining spine goes car-free every Saturday noon–10 PM through the end of September — outdoor tables, picnics and live performances down the middle of the avenue.

Carnival · Labor Day Sep 7
West Indian American Day Carnival
📍 Eastern Parkway

Carnival week runs Aug 27–Sep 7, and the main parade rolls down Eastern Parkway on Labor Day from 10 AM — mas bands, steel pan and millions of spectators, with J'Ouvert before dawn.

Bushwick

Murals After Midnight

The old German brewing district reinvented itself twice — first as a Latin American neighborhood anchored by Maria Hernandez Park, then as the city's open-air gallery, with block after block of commissioned murals around Troutman Street. After dark it runs on warehouse stages and taprooms; by day the murals do the talking.

Street Art · Free · Always On
The Bushwick Collective
📍 Troutman St around St Nicholas Ave

Lifelong resident Joe Ficalora turned his blocks into a rotating outdoor gallery for local and international muralists — start at Troutman and St Nicholas and just keep walking.

Circus Nightclub
House of Yes
📍 2 Wyckoff Ave

The costume-forward performance nightclub where aerialists share the room with the DJ — theme nights, cabaret and dance parties on a packed 2026 calendar.

The Neighborhood Park
Maria Hernandez Park
📍 Knickerbocker Ave & Starr St

Bushwick's living room — the 1890s park (once P.T. Barnum's circus lot) renamed in 1989 for the anti-drug community activist murdered that year in her Starr Street home.

Brewery Taproom
Kings County Brewers Collective
📍 381 Troutman St

Bushwick's first brewery in over forty years — hazy IPAs and tart sours from three brewers, poured in a taproom as muraled as the street outside.

Next Door · Moore St
Roberta's
📍 261 Moore St, across the East Williamsburg line

The wood-fired pizza compound that put this corner of Brooklyn on the world's food map — technically a few blocks over the city's East Williamsburg line, and inseparable from the Bushwick scene it seeded.

Coney Island

The People's Playground

America's original seaside amusement capital, still running on hot dogs, wooden coasters and ocean air at the end of the subway line. The 2026 season opened in March with the traditional egg-cream christening of the Cyclone — which turns 100 next year — and the baseball season ends right here: the Brooklyn Cyclones close the year with a Sep 1–6 homestand beside the boardwalk.

Amusement Park · 2026 Season
Luna Park
📍 1000 Surf Ave

The modern amusement park at the heart of the old playground — open daily in summer, typically 11 AM to 10 PM, with the season running from late March into the fall.

Coaster · 100 in 2027
The Cyclone
📍 Surf Ave & W 10th St

The legendary 1927 wooden coaster celebrated its 99th birthday this June — every season still opens with the egg-cream christening of its front car, and the 100th-anniversary countdown is on.

Wheel · Since 1920
Deno's Wonder Wheel
📍 3059 W 12th St

The 150-foot eccentric wheel — its swinging cars have slid on their tracks since 1920 — a city landmark that opened its 106th season with the 71st annual Blessing of the Rides.

Hot Dogs · Since 1916
Nathan's Famous — The Original
📍 Surf & Stillwell Aves

The nickel hot-dog stand that made the frankfurter a national food — open every day since 1916 on the same corner, now co-named for its founders.

Aquarium
New York Aquarium
📍 602 Surf Ave at W 8th St

The Wildlife Conservation Society's seaside aquarium on the boardwalk — sharks, sea lions and ocean conservation with the Atlantic right outside.

Baseball · Final Homestand Sep 1–6
Brooklyn Cyclones at Maimonides Park
📍 1904 Surf Ave

The Mets' High-A club plays beside the boardwalk — and for the first time since 2024 the season ends at home: a final six-game set vs. the Frederick Keys, September 1–6.

The Boardwalk
Riegelmann Boardwalk
📍 Along the beach, Sea Gate to Brighton Beach

The wooden spine of the whole show — beach on one side, rides and concessions on the other, running the length of the peninsula toward Brighton Beach.

As in Queens, these neighborhoods exist in OpenStreetMap only as point markers, not boundary relations (checked per-neighborhood via Overpass for this build), so official city shapes are used throughout: eight are NYC Dept of City Planning 2020 Neighborhood Tabulation Areas, with honest composites — Williamsburg is the Williamsburg + South Williamsburg NTAs; Prospect Heights is Prospect Heights + Prospect Park, so the Brooklyn Museum, Botanic Garden and Long Meadow fall correctly inside it; Bushwick is its West + East NTAs. DUMBO has no NTA of its own (the city lumps it with Downtown Brooklyn and Boerum Hill), so it is drawn from the only official DUMBO boundaries the city publishes: the LPC DUMBO (2007) and Fulton Ferry (1977) historic districts, and Downtown Brooklyn is the remainder of that combined NTA — which still takes in Boerum Hill. Placements follow point-in-polygon truth, not habit: Barclays Center's block, the Soldiers' & Sailors' Arch and the Grand Army Plaza Greenmarket all test into the Park Slope NTA and are listed there; the Brooklyn Paramount tests into Fort Greene, the Transit Museum into Brooklyn Heights, McCarren Pool into the Williamsburg shape, and Brooklyn Brewery's new 1 Wythe Ave home into Greenpoint. Roberta's tests just outside all ten (East Williamsburg) and is listed as an adjacency, not a placement. Every listing on this page was point-in-polygon tested against these shapes. See data source.
